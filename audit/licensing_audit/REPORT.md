## License scope and source completeness

The operative scope map is explicit in `LICENSING.md:3–12`: original prose, documents and eligible data/database rights are offered under CC-BY-4.0; original software/configuration is GPL-3.0-or-later; third-party material retains its own rights. `LICENSING.md:20–31` separates scholarly credit from legal obligations, limits grants to rights actually held, excludes ownership of mathematical facts and unprotectable raw results, and addresses software listings inside historical TeX files. No exclusive ownership of a theorem, formula, algorithm as an idea, or the Epoch problem is asserted.

The outer `LICENSE` is **byte-for-byte identical** to the official 35,149-byte GNU GPLv3 text, SHA256 `3972dc9744f6499f0f9b2dbf76696f2ae7ad8af9b23dde66d6af86c9dfb36986`. The 18,657-byte `LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt` is byte-identical to Creative Commons' official repository text, SHA256 `9ba9550ad48438d0836ddab3da480b3b69ffa0aac7b7878b5a0039e7ab429411`. See `license_text_comparison.json`, the [GNU text](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt), and the [official CC text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/creativecommons/cc-legal-tools-data/main/legacy/legalcode/by_4.0.txt).

All distributed Python programs are readable source. The main extraction runner is a 155-line standard-library script with a copyright notice and GPL-3.0-or-later SPDX identifier. The archive supplies the verification drivers, regenerator, supporting local modules, inputs, and build/reproduction instructions. Its 28 Python files generally lack per-file license headers; the outer license grant expressly includes the archive and original embedded software. Missing redundant headers does not by itself establish a GPL violation. Historical campaign scripts have frozen local-path/configuration assumptions and are labeled archival; operational portability is separate from concealment of source.

For this source-only aggregate I found no missing corresponding project source or distributed proprietary/object-code component. Imported local modules are included. External packages and engines are named and installed separately; their source trees are not bundled. The GPL distinguishes source/object conveyance, aggregate works, and program output: numerical output does not acquire GPL terms merely because a GPL program computed it. This finding does not extend to a future release that bundles dependency binaries or links newly combined components. [GNU GPLv3, sections 1–6](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.txt), [GNU FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.en.html).

The inner historical ZIP has no standalone license file. Distribution as part of this complete outer release supplies the notices. A useful nonblocking clarification in `UPLOAD_AND_REPRODUCE.md` would tell downstream users to carry `LICENSE`, `LICENSES/CC-BY-4.0.txt`, `LICENSING.md`, `NOTICE`, and third-party attribution when redistributing an extracted subset. ## Authorship, AI assistance, citation and publication metadata

The public cover, PDF Author metadata, `AUTHORS.md`, `NOTICE`, README, BibTeX, CFF and release metadata consistently identify **Maseeh Ghodsi**. `AUTHORS.md:8–12` names substantial OpenAI and Anthropic assistance in mathematical exploration, drafting, programming, checks and internal review, and disclaims the assertion that the human personally derived or checked every proof step. The cover also prominently discloses substantial AI assistance. Published theorem authors, software authors and Epoch retain their credit. No invented affiliation, ORCID or author DOI is supplied.

This is a transparent scholarly credit choice, not independent proof of the extent of human creative contribution. Provider contracts can assign their interests in output to a user/customer, subject to terms and applicable law; they cannot create copyright where no protectable authorship exists or transfer someone else's rights. The inspected [OpenAI consumer terms](https://openai.com/policies/terms-of-use/) and [services agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/), and [Anthropic consumer](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms) and [commercial terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms), do not contradict the release's qualified grant. The archive alone does not establish which account contract applied or whether the customer was the individual or another entity. The [U.S. Copyright Office's 2025 report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf) treats human contribution as a factual, case-specific issue; that is a U.S. reference, not a worldwide ruling. The release's “to the extent rights exist and are held” qualification is therefore material.
